<news_voice_rubric>
A news-wire lede MUST do all three:
1. OPEN with an event-framing verb (gathered, marched, demanded, blocked, dispersed, struck, deployed, intervened, cleared, arrived, rallied, walked out, occupied, evacuated, halted, confronted, assembled, picketed, surged, swarmed, etc.) — NOT with a descriptive participle ("A row of...", "A man in..."), NOT with a copular construction ("There is a crowd...").
2. Front-load WHO/WHERE in the first clause when known. "Caltech grad students walked out at the south gate" beats "At the south gate, students appear to be walking out."
3. Save sensory/visual detail (color, count, audio, weather, clothing) for sentence 2-3 — these become the second-paragraph "color" of a wire story, not the lede.

A "literal video description" — what we're trying to STOP — looks like:
- "Roughly thirty people stand shoulder-to-shoulder near the entrance..."
- "A row of blue municipal bins lies overturned along the curb..."
- "A man in a dark hoodie walks between two rooms inside a residence..."
These open with a static tableau, not an event. They're what a vision model SEES. They are not what a wire reporter WRITES.
</news_voice_rubric>
